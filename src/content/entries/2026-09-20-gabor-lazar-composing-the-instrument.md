# Gábor Lázár: Composing the Instrument

Gábor Lázár has been working in Max/MSP for around fifteen years, and in recent years it has become his entire studio. His approach to composition centers on "compositional interfaces" — systems he builds where sound emerges from the interactions within them rather than being engineered through traditional synthesis approaches.

## The Studio Environment

For Lázár, Max is not just a tool; it's a **complete compositional environment**. Rather than arranging tracks in a DAW, he builds the instrument first and composes by playing it.

> "In the last couple [of years] Max has been literally the only thing I use. Of course, I'm using VST synthesisers within Max, but what I really like about it is that I can create an environment where I'm not creating tracks but instruments."
> — [Gábor Lázár, interviewed by Danny Turner, *MusicRadar*, 2024](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/theres-constant-hype-about-the-instruments-synths-speakers-and-computers-that-producers-use-but-will-your-music-sound-better-because-of-that-gabor-lazar-on-sound-design-generative-techniques-and-designing-his-own-instruments-in-max)

He contrasts this directly with conventional software:

> "That's a whole different approach to music making because if you use Logic, Cubase or Ableton, every time you sit down you start from scratch… Max/MSP is more like going to a workshop or laboratory."
> — [Turner, *MusicRadar*, 2024](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/theres-constant-hype-about-the-instruments-synths-speakers-and-computers-that-producers-use-but-will-your-music-sound-better-because-of-that-gabor-lazar-on-sound-design-generative-techniques-and-designing-his-own-instruments-in-max)

## Organizing Sound Rather Than Synthesizing It

Lázár's compositional approach emphasizes organizing sound rather than engineering it. He focuses on the **system** that produces the sound rather than the individual sonic artifacts.

> "I'm more into organising sound and making Max/MSP patches that represent a score or skeleton of the music."
> — [Turner, *MusicRadar*, 2024](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/theres-constant-hype-about-the-instruments-synths-speakers-and-computers-that-producers-use-but-will-your-music-sound-better-because-of-that-gabor-lazar-on-sound-design-generative-techniques-and-designing-his-own-instruments-in-max)

This means creating systems where audio and musical content emerges from the interactions between elements, much like how natural systems generate complex behavior from simple rules.

## Deconstruction Rather Than Arrangement

The interface was built specifically to work against the grain of commercial software, which lays musical elements out along a timeline.

> "I would like to work in the opposite way to this type of software. While music-making software is designed to arrange musical elements linearly, my interface was designed to deconstruct them in a non-linear way."
> — [Gábor Lázár, interviewed by Matt Mullen, *MusicRadar*, 2022](https://www.musicradar.com/news/gabor-lazar-interview)

In live performance this deconstruction is pushed further still:

> "When I play live I interact with rhythmic patterns and textures and usually I go further with the deconstruction."
> — [Mullen, *MusicRadar*, 2022](https://www.musicradar.com/news/gabor-lazar-interview)

## Algorithmic vs Generative Approaches

Lázár is careful about the word "generative", and rejects the idea that the system composes on its own:

> "I'm not using generative tools, I'm using generative techniques. Humans cannot be excluded from the process of making music."
> — [Turner, *MusicRadar*, 2024](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/theres-constant-hype-about-the-instruments-synths-speakers-and-computers-that-producers-use-but-will-your-music-sound-better-because-of-that-gabor-lazar-on-sound-design-generative-techniques-and-designing-his-own-instruments-in-max)

He puts a number on it, and prefers a different label altogether:

> "If you listen to my music, 90% of it is my personality and 10% is based on generative tricks. […] I prefer to call it algorithmic music."
> — [Turner, *MusicRadar*, 2024](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/theres-constant-hype-about-the-instruments-synths-speakers-and-computers-that-producers-use-but-will-your-music-sound-better-because-of-that-gabor-lazar-on-sound-design-generative-techniques-and-designing-his-own-instruments-in-max)

## Building What Doesn't Exist Yet

The motivation for building the interface was partly a frustration with what commercial instruments offer.

> "Why do most synths, drum machines, software or any music technology still offer technically almost the same techniques that we already had 30-40 years ago?"
> — [Mullen, *MusicRadar*, 2022](https://www.musicradar.com/news/gabor-lazar-interview)

> "My intention was to build an interface that would allow me to create structures that would be very complicated or impossible to make by using widely available software."
> — [Mullen, *MusicRadar*, 2022](https://www.musicradar.com/news/gabor-lazar-interview)

## Recording Methodology

Lázár's albums track the instruments he was building at the time — the record is a document of the interface as much as of the music.

> "My early albums reflected the recording of the instruments I made in the same way that later albums are representative of my current compositional interface."
> — [Turner, *MusicRadar*, 2024](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/theres-constant-hype-about-the-instruments-synths-speakers-and-computers-that-producers-use-but-will-your-music-sound-better-because-of-that-gabor-lazar-on-sound-design-generative-techniques-and-designing-his-own-instruments-in-max)

Recording is fast and intuitive rather than laboured, despite being entirely computer-based:

> "Even though it's computer music, things happen intuitively when I interact with my interface, decisions are made quickly when recording."
> — [Mullen, *MusicRadar*, 2022](https://www.musicradar.com/news/gabor-lazar-interview)

*Boundary Object* (Planet Mu, 2022) collects tracks recorded in real time through one such self-designed interface.

## Performance as a Physical Event

The live set is not only a rendering of the composition — volume and physical impact are part of the material.

> "For me playing live is not just about the performance and the composition but it's about loudness and physical aspects."
> — [Mullen, *MusicRadar*, 2022](https://www.musicradar.com/news/gabor-lazar-interview)

> "All of the sequences, patterns and loops are saved as presets and when I play tracks I'm playing drum samples and synths in the same way that I do in the studio, which is almost 100% live."
> — [Turner, *MusicRadar*, 2024](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/theres-constant-hype-about-the-instruments-synths-speakers-and-computers-that-producers-use-but-will-your-music-sound-better-because-of-that-gabor-lazar-on-sound-design-generative-techniques-and-designing-his-own-instruments-in-max)

## Why Max/MSP?

For all these approaches to work, Lázár needed a tool that would let him build custom systems rather than rely on standard presets:

- Complete control over every aspect of the signal processing
- Ability to create interactive and responsive compositions
- Flexibility to experiment and evolve his setups

> "I didn't want to buy something that most producers already have and use. In Max/MSP, I can create my own musical language."
> — [Mullen, *MusicRadar*, 2022](https://www.musicradar.com/news/gabor-lazar-interview)

> "I think I just like to be independent and create my own universe of things."
> — [Mullen, *MusicRadar*, 2022](https://www.musicradar.com/news/gabor-lazar-interview)
